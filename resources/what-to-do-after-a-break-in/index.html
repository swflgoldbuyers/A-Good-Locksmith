What to Do After a Break-In: A Locksmith's Guide

8 min read · Updated 2026-03-05

  1. Home
  2. Resources
  3. What to Do After a Break-In: A Locksmith's Guide

A break-in leaves more than damaged hardware. It unsettles the feeling that home is under your control. In the first hours, the priorities are safety, evidence, and getting the property secured again so you can sleep, shower, and think clearly.

A Good Locksmith has helped homeowners and businesses across Southwest Florida recover from forced entry since 1994. This guide focuses on practical next steps: what to do immediately, how to board up or re-secure doors, and how to rebuild the entry stronger than before with emergency locksmith support and lasting lock installation.

There is no perfect emotional script for the day your door gets forced. There is a practical sequence that keeps you safer and makes repairs more effective. Follow the order even if friends are already offering to nail a board up or bring spare locks from their garage.

Make Sure the Scene Is Safe First

If you arrive home and suspect an intruder may still be inside, do not enter. Call 911 from a safe location and wait for law enforcement. The same rule applies if a door is open, lights are on that should not be, or you hear movement.

Once officers confirm the home is clear, you can begin documentation and repairs. If anyone is injured or highly distressed, handle medical and personal needs before worrying about hardware. Locks matter, but people come first.

Neighbors in Fort Myers and Cape Coral sometimes discover damage hours after the event. Even then, treat the property carefully until you know the status of the scene.

If you rent, notify property management as soon as the scene is safe and documented. If you own, notify your insurer according to your policy requirements. Clear communication early prevents delays when receipts and photos are needed.

Document Damage Before Cleaning Everything Away

Photograph the affected door, frame, locks, windows, and any disturbed rooms before major cleanup. Those images help with police reports and insurance claims, and they help a locksmith understand how the entry happened.

  • Photograph wide shots and close-ups of damaged hardware
  • Note the time you discovered the break-in
  • List missing items as soon as you can inventory them
  • Keep damaged locks if your insurer or investigator may want to see them

Do not assume a door that still closes is secure. Kick damage often compromises the jamb even when the slab looks usable.

Write down serial numbers of damaged smart devices or cameras if relevant. Digital evidence and hardware details can matter later, and they are easy to forget once cleanup begins.

Secure the Opening the Same Day

An unsecured home after a break-in is vulnerable to a second visit. Call a professional locksmith for same-day securing: temporary reinforcements, lock replacement, cylinder changes, or board-up coordination depending on the damage.

In many cases we replace the compromised deadbolt, reinforce the strike area, and rekey remaining exterior locks so old keys and damaged hardware are no longer part of the system. If the door slab or frame is too damaged for a normal lock to hold, we will tell you honestly that door repair or replacement needs to come first.

Homeowners comparing hardware options can review Choosing the Right Deadbolt for Your Home once the emergency phase settles.

Ask the locksmith to evaluate adjacent openings, not only the damaged door. Thieves sometimes test a side door or window before finding the weak point they ultimately used. A same-day perimeter check is time well spent.

Upgrade the Weak Point Instead of Matching the Old Setup

The goal is not to recreate the exact lock that failed. The goal is to harden the entry method that was exploited. That may mean a stronger deadbolt, a reinforced strike plate with longer screws, a better door wrap, or moving away from a weak knob-only setup.

If several exterior doors still use outdated hardware, consider upgrading them together. Repeat offenders and opportunistic thieves look for easy second chances. Our guide to replacement signs can help you evaluate the rest of the house.

Business owners should also review access after a commercial break-in. Employee keys, rear exit hardware, and suite doors may need a broader reset through commercial locksmith services.

Choose hardware for durability in Florida conditions, not just matching the old finish. A shiny lock that corrodes quickly is not a meaningful upgrade. Discuss graded deadbolts and reinforced strikes as a package.

Reset Access for Everyone Who Should Still Have a Key

After forced entry, re-evaluate who holds keys or codes. Even if the break-in was pure opportunism, a fresh access plan restores confidence. Rekey remaining good cylinders, issue new keys deliberately, and retire old copies.

If you use a smart lock or keypad, remove unfamiliar codes and create new ones for trusted people only. Travelers and seasonal residents can fold that reset into the habits from How to Secure Your Home While Traveling.

Landlords recovering a rental unit should follow a turnover-style checklist, including the steps in Rental Property Locksmith Checklist for Landlords.

If employees, contractors, or roommates are part of your access circle, reset codes and keys with a short written list. Stressful weeks are when people accidentally leave old codes active.

Rebuild Confidence With a Full Perimeter Walk

Once the attacked door is solid again, walk the rest of the property in daylight. Check windows, yard gates, exterior lighting, ladder storage, and whether shrubs give cover near entries. Many homeowners only harden the damaged door and leave an easier window latch untouched.

Ask your locksmith practical questions while on site: Is this door grade adequate? Should the garage entry match the front door? Is keyed-alike convenience still wise, or do you want a higher-security keyway now?

A Good Locksmith serves Naples, Lehigh Acres, Estero, and nearby communities with calm, clear repair plans. You do not have to figure out every product name on the worst day of the month. Call, secure the opening, and upgrade with intention.

Consider lighting and landscaping changes in the following week, once emergency repairs are complete. Motion lighting by a side yard and trimmed shrubs near doors improve daily security without turning the house into a fortress. Communities from Bonita Springs to Cape Coral see the same pattern: strong locks plus fewer hiding spots near entries.

Give yourself permission to stage repairs in phases: secure tonight, upgrade this week, refine landscaping and lighting afterward. Trying to solve every security idea on day one creates decision fatigue. A locked, reinforced door and controlled keys are the non-negotiables; everything else can follow with a clearer head.
