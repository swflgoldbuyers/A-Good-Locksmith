How Often Should You Rekey Your Home?

7 min read · Updated 2026-01-15

  1. Home
  2. Resources
  3. How Often Should You Rekey Your Home?

Rekeying is one of the fastest, most affordable ways to restore control of your home's locks. Instead of replacing every lockset, a trained locksmith changes the pins inside the cylinder so old keys stop working and a new key operates the door. For homeowners across Fort Myers, Cape Coral, and the rest of Southwest Florida, knowing when to rekey can prevent costly surprises later.

At A Good Locksmith, we have helped local families and landlords make that decision since 1994. There is no single calendar date that fits every house, but there are clear moments when rekeying should move to the top of your security checklist. This guide explains those moments, how the process works, and when a full lock replacement is the wiser upgrade.

Southwest Florida homes see a lot of key sharing: landscapers, pool service, contractors after storms, and relatives watching the house during hurricane season. Each extra copy increases the chance that an old key is still in circulation long after the relationship ends. Rekeying resets that uncertainty without requiring a full hardware overhaul on every door.

What Rekeying Actually Changes

When we rekey a lock, we keep your existing hardware on the door and rebuild the cylinder with a new pin combination. Your doors still look the same, but every previous key becomes useless. That is ideal when the lock body is solid and you mainly need to cut off unauthorized access.

Rekeying is different from changing locks. Changing locks means installing new deadbolts or knobsets, which is the right move when hardware is worn, outdated, or no longer grade-rated for residential security. If you are unsure which path fits your home, our residential locksmith team can inspect the cylinders and recommend rekeying or replacement on the spot.

  • Old keys no longer open the door after a rekey
  • New keys can often be keyed alike so one key works every exterior door
  • Cost is typically lower than buying and installing all-new hardware
  • Most standard pin-tumbler locks can be rekeyed the same day

Most pin-tumbler residential locks respond well to professional rekeying when the cylinder is not worn out. If the plug turns roughly even with the correct key, or if the lock has been previously forced, your technician may recommend replacement instead of another pin change. That on-site judgment is one reason local service beats mailing locks away or guessing from a hardware-aisle chart.

Clear Moments When You Should Rekey

After you move into a new home. You rarely know who still has keys from previous owners, contractors, or real estate showings. Rekeying on move-in day is one of the simplest security habits we recommend to every buyer in Estero, Bonita Springs, and Naples.

When keys are lost or stolen. A missing spare is more than an inconvenience if someone can identify your address. Rekey promptly rather than hoping the key never resurfaces. If you also need emergency access today, pair rekeying with our emergency locksmith response.

After roommate, caregiver, or contractor turnover. Anyone who had legitimate access and no longer should is a reason to reset the key system. The same rule applies after a separation or divorce when physical keys are still in circulation.

Between rental tenants. Landlords serving Lehigh Acres and Cape Coral should rekey as a standard turnover step. It protects the new resident and limits liability if a former tenant kept a copy. See our related checklist in Rental Property Locksmith Checklist for Landlords.

Real estate closings in Lee and Collier County often leave a trail of temporary access. Cleaning crews, inspectors, and agents may have needed entry during the transaction. Even when everyone acted professionally, you still have no inventory of every copy. A move-in rekey closes that chapter cleanly before furniture day chaos begins.

How Often Is Often Enough?

If none of the events above have happened, many homeowners still benefit from reviewing keys every two to three years. Count how many copies exist, who holds them, and whether any exterior lock is sticky, loose, or hard to turn. A quiet annual security walk-through catches problems before a lock fails on a stormy night.

Homes with frequent guest access, vacation use, or multiple adult children coming and going may need more frequent rekeys. Seasonal residents in Southwest Florida often rekey when they return for the winter season, especially if the property was rented or watched by others while they were away. For travel-season planning, read How to Secure Your Home While Traveling.

There is no penalty for rekeying sooner than a fixed schedule. The goal is control of access, not waiting for a round-number anniversary.

Keep a simple key log on your phone: who has a copy, when it was issued, and whether it was returned. The log will not replace a rekey after a true loss, but it prevents the common problem of cutting five more keys every year because nobody remembers what already exists.

When Replacement Beats Rekeying

Rekeying cannot fix every problem. If the lock is cracked, heavily corroded from coastal humidity, drilled from a break-in, or using obsolete residential-grade hardware, replacement is the better investment. Older knob-only entry sets without a deadbolt are another upgrade moment; rekeying a weak lock still leaves a weak door.

We often recommend upgrading to a solid deadbolt at the same visit. Learn more in Choosing the Right Deadbolt for Your Home and 5 Signs It's Time To Replace Your Locks. Homeowners comparing electronic options can also review Smart Locks vs Traditional Locks.

Our technicians serving Naples and Bonita Springs regularly combine rekey and upgrade work in one appointment so you are not scheduling multiple visits.

Coastal corrosion can make a lock look fine outside while the internal pins become gritty. If rekeying restores key control but the cylinder still feels rough, ask about upgrading that door during the same visit. One upgraded deadbolt on the main entry often delivers more security than rekeying a collection of tired builder-grade knobs.

What to Expect During a Professional Rekey

A typical residential rekey starts with confirming which doors you want on the new key. We remove the cylinders, install a new pin combination, test smooth operation, and cut the number of keys you request. Most standard homes are completed in a single visit with minimal disruption.

  • Identify every exterior door, garage entry, and storage room you want included
  • Decide whether doors should be keyed alike or kept on separate keys
  • Ask about restricted keyways if you want blank keys that are harder to duplicate casually
  • Test each door after the rekey before the technician leaves

If you are locked out before the rekey can happen, call for a safe entry first, then reset the cylinders. Drivers who face a similar scramble with vehicle keys can start with Locked Out of Your Car? Here's What To Do.

Ask for keys cut from quality blanks and test each one before the technician leaves. A key that works on the workbench but binds in a slightly misaligned door will frustrate you later. Good rekey service includes door-by-door verification, not just cylinder work on a table.

A Practical Rekey Checklist for Southwest Florida Homes

Use this short list after any major household change: moving day, lost keys, ended relationships, contractor projects, or tenant turnover. Walk the property, note every lock that accepts a key, and decide whether those locks should share one new key. Include sliding door locks, side gates, and shed locks if they use the same style of cylinder.

Then schedule professional rekeying before the next busy week swallows the task. Neighbors in Estero and San Carlos Park often bundle the visit with a quick hardware inspection so sticky locks and loose strike plates get corrected at the same time.

Rekeying is not dramatic, and that is the point. It is a quiet, practical reset that puts you back in charge of who can walk through your front door. When you are ready, A Good Locksmith is here with the same straightforward service Southwest Florida has trusted since 1994.
