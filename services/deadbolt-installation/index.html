Single-Cylinder, Double-Cylinder, and Smart Deadbolts

Most homes use single-cylinder deadbolts that key from the outside and thumb-turn from the inside. They offer a strong balance of security and emergency egress. Double-cylinder deadbolts key on both sides and are sometimes requested for doors with adjacent glass, but they require careful consideration for fire-exit safety and local practicality.

Electronic and smart lock deadbolts add keypad or app access while still providing a deadbolt mechanism. If you are deciding between traditional and connected hardware, read smart locks vs. traditional locks.

Installation Quality Is the Real Security Feature

Even a high-quality deadbolt underperforms if the strike plate is held by short screws into thin trim. We pay attention to how the bolt seats in the frame, whether the door has excessive play, and whether longer screws or a reinforced strike should be part of the job. Smooth throwing of the bolt is a sign the door and lock are working together.

Coastal humidity in places like Bonita Springs and Naples can swell doors seasonally. During installation we set hardware so normal movement does not immediately create binding. If an existing deadbolt sticks, our lock repair service may solve it without a full replacement.

When Homeowners Upgrade Deadbolts

Common triggers include buying a home, noticing weak or mismatched hardware, preparing for seasonal travel, or responding to a neighborhood break-in. Deadbolt upgrades are also popular when French doors or patio doors need better security than a simple latch provides.

After an incident, combine hardware upgrades with the steps in what to do after a break-in. Before a trip, review how to secure your home while traveling and test every deadbolt. If keys are unaccounted for, pair new deadbolts with lock rekeying on any locks you keep.
