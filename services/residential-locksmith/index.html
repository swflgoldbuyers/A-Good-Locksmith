Complete Home Locksmith Solutions

Residential security is not one-size-fits-all. A condo in downtown Fort Myers has different needs than a single-family home in Lehigh Acres or a gated community in Naples. We evaluate your entry points, existing hardware, and lifestyle so recommendations fit how you actually live.

  • Home lockouts handled carefully to avoid damage whenever possible
  • Lock rekeying so old keys no longer work
  • Lock repair for sticky, worn, or failing hardware
  • Deadbolt installation for stronger door security
  • New lock installation and hardware upgrades
  • Mailbox lock replacement and keyed alike options

If you are unsure whether to repair, rekey, or replace, we walk you through the practical differences. Many Southwest Florida homeowners are surprised how often a rekey restores security without the cost of full hardware replacement. Learn more in our guide on how often you should rekey your home.

When Homeowners Call a Residential Locksmith

Common situations we handle every week across Lee and Collier County include moving into a new home, tenant or roommate turnover, lost or stolen keys, sticky doors after humidity and storm seasons, and planned security upgrades before travel. Seasonal residents often schedule service before leaving for the summer; year-round families call after remodeling, garage door changes, or when a door no longer latches cleanly.

After a break-in or attempted break-in, speed matters. Compromised locks should be addressed promptly so unauthorized keys or damaged hardware cannot be used again. Our article on what to do after a break-in outlines practical next steps, and we can rekey or replace locks the same day in many cases.

Stronger Hardware for Southwest Florida Homes

Coastal humidity, sun exposure, and salty air can wear finishes and internal lock parts faster than in drier climates. Choosing corrosion-resistant finishes and properly rated deadbolts helps locks last longer and operate smoothly through Florida summers.

We install and service popular residential brands and help you select Grade 1 or Grade 2 deadbolts suited to exterior doors. If you are comparing options, start with choosing the right deadbolt.
